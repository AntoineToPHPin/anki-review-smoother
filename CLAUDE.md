# Review Smoother — context for agents

Anki add-on ("Review Smoother" on AnkiWeb; repo `anki-review-smoother`) that reshapes each deck's upcoming review forecast into a smooth descending curve — e.g. 4/day, then 3/day, then 2/day — instead of a jagged 5, 2, 4, 3… It only moves due dates; it never changes how many cards there are.

The author discusses in French; everything in the repo (code, comments, README, UI strings) is English because the add-on is meant for wide sharing. The UI is translated to French (see `i18n.py`).

> **Keep this file up to date** without being asked: remove open items once resolved, add durable conventions, design decisions and gotchas. Only what someone resuming cold needs — not task-by-task progress.

## Design decisions (agreed with the author)

- **Target curve**: two methods, chosen per deck in the dialog (« Méthode », remembered in config `decks`): automatic regular staircase (`smooth`, the default) or a **custom curve** (`custom_curve` + `follow_curve`, asked 2026-11-13). A third one, only fill the gaps (`fill_gaps`), was removed at the author's request on 2026-11-16 (a deck saved with it opens with "auto"). A manual plateau list (`4x14, 3x28…`) had been dropped on 2026-11-11; what the author actually uses is the parametric curve of their spreadsheet `C:\Users\AntoineBeSTT\Documents\Logiciels\PERSONNEL\Planification musique.xlsx` (read 2026-11-12, see below), now built in.
- **Custom curve** (the author's spreadsheet, reproduced exactly — `CustomCurveTest` checks its totals 1002 / 1880 / 4721 for `55, 4, 3, 13, 19.01, 194`): day 1 = `tomorrow`; day 2 = `tomorrow − drop_day2`; days 3–90 = `floor + ⌊(tomorrow − drop_day2 − drop_day3 − floor) × e^(−(day−3)/strength)⌋` (Ebbinghaus forgetting curve from Wikipedia, `strength` = « Force de la mémoire »); after day 90 = `round(floor − a × (day − 90))`, `a` entered × 10,000 as in the sheet (`slope_x10000`, 194). The sheet's b (13) equals its `floor` (« Quantité révision à long terme »), so b is not a separate setting; its « Nouvelles cartes apprises » column is a log only. Author's answers (2026-11-13): **strength and slope entered by hand**, not solved for (the dialog shows the curve's total vs the cards due, as the sheet's totals did, so they tune it); tomorrow and the two drops are **comfort targets**, not read from Anki; **surplus is postponed** (the curve is followed exactly); settings **per deck**. On 2026-11-13 `Musique::Paroles de chansons` was exactly on the sheet's curve (981 / 1880 / 4721 cards over 30 / 90 / 365 days; 0, 0 and 1 card to move): the author keeps it there by hand.
- **Moves**: cards are almost always *advanced*. Postponing is the exception (decided 2026-11-11): margin **10% of the interval**, **longest intervals first**, budget **1% of the window's cards (at least 1)**, used **only to lower the peak at the start of the curve**. **Off by default since 2026-11-12** (author's choice, `allow_postpone` config, was on before), with a dialog checkbox that recomputes the preview instantly. On `Paroles de chansons` it postpones 1 card from day 1 to day 2 for a peak of 54 instead of 55: the author keeps that behaviour (no minimum-gain threshold) but wants the option unchecked.
- **Horizon**: 90 days by default, configurable. Day 1 = tomorrow; today, overdue, new, learning, suspended and buried cards are never touched.
- **Window edge** (decided 2026-11-12): cards due in the **next quarter of the horizon** after it (`LOOKAHEAD = 0.25`, `lookahead_end(horizon)`: 90 → up to day 112, 30 → day 37) may be **pulled into the window** (advanced), so the end of the window does not drop just because the cards due right after it are left out (`Histoire`: days 89–90 were empty with cards due on days 93 and 96). Which cards actually come in changed on 2026-11-14 (see the next point). The author chose the quarter after comparing with "as long as the horizon" (pulls more, from further away — e.g. lengthens Bépo's plateau of 2s) and "10% of the horizon" (only the very edge). Accepted consequence: on sparse decks the block of 1s extends into the trailing 0s (PHP: 4 cards pulled). On Bépo that made a `1x32` tail, which led to evening out down to 0 (`even_out`, replaced on 2026-11-16 by `regular_staircase`, which keeps the 1s).
- **Days after the window** (asked 2026-11-14): taking a card from the day right after the window (J91 for 90 days, J366 for a year) leaves a gap that shows in the window **the next day**, when that day becomes its last one (« ça fausse les stats le lendemain IRL »); putting one there (custom curve's postponed cards, which went to horizon + 1), a peak. Options discussed: (1) take only from days after the window **above a reference**, the nearest first, carried in by hops; (2) never take from outside, leave the window short; (3) take from as far as possible; the author chose **1, with 3 as a fallback**. `_outside_target` builds a target for every day of the loaded cards: the window's, then after it each day keeps its count, except days above the reference, lowered (nearest first) by the cards the window lacks; when the window has too many (custom curve), days below the reference are raised, nearest first. Reference: the custom curve carried on after the window (`custom_curve(settings, outside_end(horizon))`), else `levels()` of the days after the window. Fallback when no day is above (below) it: one card a day from `lookahead_end` backwards. Cards are loaded up to `outside_end(horizon)` = 2 × horizon (`OUTSIDE = 1.0`: `Paroles de chansons` over a year had no surplus before J477). `smooth` (and `fill_gaps`, removed since) still compute their window curve from the cards up to `lookahead_end`; only where the pulled cards come from changed. The assignment (`assign_in_short_hops` / `assign_both_ways`, then `fewer_moves`) runs on that whole target, so cards hop through days after the window, each keeping its count; `_few_moves` no longer carries a gap past the first later day with a card to spare (it would leave a gap nothing fills, and the pass would fail). Real decks 2026-11-14: `Paroles de chansons` 365 days, 6 cards short → taken from J380, J407, J477–J480 (all above the curve), 58 cards moved ≤ 5 %; `Pistes d'album` 90 days → J92 (2 cards, level 1), J91 untouched; `Bépo` 90 days → J96, J105, J112–J114.
- **Trigger**: manual only — a menu action opening a dialog with a before/after preview, confirmation, and undo.
- **Reposition new cards** (asked 2026-11-17, second dialog, Tools menu « Repositionner les nouvelles cartes… »): the author renumbers new cards daily. Anki's own Reposition gives **every card of a note the same position**, and its « Ordre aléatoire » shuffles **notes**, not cards (seen on `Bépo::Caractères`: 16 new cards, 2 notes with two). Mode « à la suite » (done): one position per card, `start`, `start + step`…, in the current order (position, note, template) or shuffled **card by card**; shuffled, **sibling cards are kept at least as many positions apart as the deck's new cards per day** (the author chose « écarter les sœurs » over a pure shuffle; options' `new.perDay`, 6 on Bépo), so they are not introduced the same day; if impossible, as far apart as possible (gap tried from that value down: random slot-by-slot attempts, then a greedy « note with the most cards left » that reaches the best gap — checked against every permutation of small decks; the greedy alone would always put siblings first, hence random first). Writes `card.due` directly (one undo step), never Anki's reposition. Start/step/shuffle remembered per deck (config `reposition`). Mode « par blocs » (asked 2026-11-17, `block_positions`) for `Musique::Paroles de chansons`: one song per block of 100 positions — difficult songs from 11, 101… 401 (a sixth optional at 501), medium 601 (701 optional), easy 801 and 901, bonus 1001. A song's level is the author's own choice, **not stored in Anki and they don't want it added**; when a song is finished, the songs of the same level move up a block (by hand, not daily); positions 1–10 stay free to put a few cards to learn right away. The daily task is only **moving each block's cards up to its start** (cards at 103… → 101…), and the author asked for just that, generically: blocks of `block_size` (100) positions, the first from `first_block` (11), a card's block given by its current position (`block_start`), cards renumbered from the block's start in their current order, one position per card; cards before `first_block` untouched. **Songs longer than a block** (common, in any block, author 2026-11-17: `Rap God` 122 cards from 1001): when a group's cards reach its block's last position and the next block's cards follow without a gap (100 → 101), they are one group, moved up as a whole from its first block's start (first version split them by block, leaving a gap: refused). A group only moves earlier within the positions it spans, so groups never mix. Known ambiguity, accepted: a song exactly filling its block (90 cards from 11, 100 from 101) right before the next song would be merged with it; a gap at the boundary (cards moved by hand to 1–10) splits a long song.
- **Tracking in Anki** (asked 2026-11-18): the author kept, one row a day, their custom curve's settings and totals in the sheets « Suivi 90 j. » (848 days) and « Suivi 365 j. » (621 days) of `Planification musique.xlsx`, and wanted them in Anki, **visible in AnkiDroid** (add-on config, `meta.json`, does not sync). Stored in **one note per tracked deck** (note type « Review Smoother – Suivi », fields Paquet + Historique; deck « Review Smoother »; card **suspended**, so never studied nor counted in the tracked deck; opened from the Browser's preview, on the phone too). The history is JSON in the field (`tracking.py`); the card template draws, with plain JS, **a graph and a table** (the author's choice): one tab per sheet, a column picker, ranges 1 month / 3 months / 1 year / all, hover/touch tooltip, table newest first. The sheets' two tables are kept as they are: their overlapping columns differ (90 j. « Total à un an » 4725 vs 365 j. 4721; the 365 sheet's « Quantité total à un an » is the typed target « Cible à un an », not computed). Sheet facts: « Total à un mois » = curve over **31** days (1002); « Total paquet » = cards not new (`-is:new`, 12947); « Nouvelles cartes apprises » = `introduced:1`; « ? » = unknown, kept as typed; cell comments (« Correction de « … » ») became a « Remarque » column. Rows had no dates: the last one is 2026-11-18; the author thought one a day without gaps, but **days are missing**. « Suivi 90 j. » was dated against the review log (new cards per day and the learned total, revlog day = 4 a.m. rollover): 4 days missing (2024-08-17, 2025-08-15, 2025-12-01, 2026-11-15; the next row often holds both days), first row 2024-07-20. « Suivi 365 j. » was aligned on it (its « Total à un an » = the custom curve of the 90 j. row's settings with its own slope, its « Total à trois mois » = the 90 j. one): ~34 days missing, first row 2025-02-02; a row where values change keeps its aligned date, the following ones go day by day (inside an unchanged plateau the missing day cannot be located). Corrections asked by the author (2026-11-18): 90 j. « Total à un an » was 4725 from 2026-09-24 (a typo, never right) → 4730 on 09-24 (slope 192), 4721 from 09-25 (slope 194); 365 j. « Quantité total à un an » stuck at 2856 from 2026-09-15 → `Total à un an − Total à trois mois + Quantité à 90 jours − Quantité à 365 jours` (its formula elsewhere; 2846 from 09-25). Imported with `tools/import_tracking.py`, then re-imported corrected and re-dated by a one-off script (not kept) on 2026-11-18. **Recording** (author's choice 2026-11-18, not automatic): « Enregistrer le suivi du jour… » button in the Review Smoother dialog (enabled for the custom curve only) → `tracking_ui.TrackingDialog`: Anki's day (`sched.day_cutoff`), one editable field per value, prefilled from the curve shown in the dialog (not necessarily saved) and the deck, plus « Remarque »; saving writes that day's row in **every tab** (replacing it if recorded already), creating note type/note on a deck's first record. Columns are matched to values **by their label** (`tracking.column_keys`: `i18n` `tracking_col_<key>` in every language — the French ones are the spreadsheet headers — plus `ALIASES`, « Coefficient b » = floor; spaces/case ignored), so the imported tabs need no extra data; a column nobody knows gets its own field, prefilled from the day's row. Values as the spreadsheet: month = 31 days, « Quantité total à un an » = curve days 90–364, `new_cards` = `introduced:1`, `learned` = `-is:new` (checked: the 2026-11-18 row comes out identical). Merging the two tabs' duplicate columns: later.
- **Preview graph**: must look like Anki's Statistics "Future Due" graph (French UI: « Charge de travail », ftl key `statistics-future-due-title`). Reimplement it — do not copy Anki's code (AGPL; this repo is MIT).
- Only the due date changes; the interval is kept.

## Algorithm (`smoothing.py`)

- `auto_curve(counts)`: upper concave hull of the cumulative due counts = the lowest non-increasing *fractional* daily rate that never falls behind what is due (so advancing alone is always enough). Rounded **down** to whole cards on the cumulative curve — still ≥ the integer due counts, and moves cards less than rounding up. The alternating rounding (4, 3, 4, 3… / 1, 0, 1, 1, 0…) is then sorted descending into a staircase; sorting only moves cards earlier, so the constraint holds. **Sparse decks (< 1 card/day) end as a block of 1s followed by 0s — the author's explicit choice** (2026-11-10), rather than spacing 1 card every n days or a density that keeps decreasing below 1/day. Downside: on such decks some cards are advanced a large share of their interval (up to ~50% seen on `Informatique::PHP`). Accepted as is: the user picks which decks to smooth, and would not smooth a deck that sparse — so no max-advance limit for now.
- `smooth(cards, horizon, allow_postpone)`: entry point used by the UI and `simulate.py`. `cards` = cards due on days 1..`outside_end(horizon)`; returns the curve over the window only (its sum includes the pulled cards) and a new due for every card. The curve is computed from the cards up to `lookahead_end` and only its first `horizon` days are used: its window total is kept, the curve itself is `regular_staircase(window due counts, that total)`, then `_outside_target` extends it after the window (pulled cards come from days with cards to spare) and the assignment runs on that whole target. Postponing only uses window cards (still capped at the horizon, so they never leave the window), budget = 1% of window cards, and the step rule looks at `[:horizon]` only. A pile right after the window can hold the peak up, so the postpone loop also stops when lowering does not lower `target[0]` (without that check it looped forever). Starts from `auto_curve`; if allowed, tries to lower the first value (the peak) one card/day at a time via `_postpone_to_cap` (forward sweep: whenever cards due on days 1..k exceed cap·k, push one card a day later — preferably one already pushed, to postpone few distinct cards; else longest interval; tie → due latest, to push it the fewest days), keeping each lowering only while the postponed count stays within budget **and the lowered curve has no steeper day-to-day step than the advance-only curve** (added 2026-11-11 after the author found `Bépo::Caractères` much better without postponing: capping its steady slope 18, 17, 17, 16, 15… gave a 16 × 7-day plateau then a cliff to 13), (a top-plateau rule, `_top_plateau_too_long`, added 2026-11-12, was removed with `even_out` on 2026-11-16: a regular staircase never has a top plateau longer than the next). Postponing is for isolated bumps, not for flattening steady slopes. Since 2026-11-16 the postponed cards are kept only if the regular staircase of the shifted cards starts lower than without postponing (otherwise a card was postponed for nothing: `[5, 4, 4, 4, 4, 2…]` gave the same curve with one more card moved). Then `assign_in_short_hops` on the shifted due days (was `assign` until 2026-11-13).
  - **Rejected first attempt** (don't retry): the lowest curve reachable with every card allowed its full 10% margin. It reshapes the whole curve and postponed 67/160 and 296/420 cards on real decks — not an exception. With the final rules (data of 2026-11-12): 1 card on `Paroles de chansons (entières)` (removes an isolated 4), 1 on `Bépo::Caractères` (18 → 17), none on `Paroles de chansons` or `Pays`.
- `regular_staircase(due, total)` (asked 2026-11-16, replaces `even_out`): the curve of `smooth`. The author found `Bépo::Caractères` over 90 days irregular (`16×3, 15×5, 14×4, 13×1, 12×2…` after `even_out`) and said they would rather raise J1 to 18 and J2–J3 to 17; asked for **a generic rule for every deck**, they chose the **strict rule** and **least total advance**. Rule (`is_regular` in the tests): going down, **each plateau lasts at least as long as the one above it**, steps of **one card**; the last plateau (cut by the window end) and the 0s are free; between **one-day plateaus** only (steep start, `Paroles` 57, 53, 49…) the curve may drop by more than one card, never more than the step before (`6, 4, 4` is a cliff: refused, found by a test). Reachable by advancing only (cumulative ≥ due every day), total = the window's cards + those `auto_curve` pulls in. Among those, J1 included, the one with the **least total advance** (Σ over days of cumulative target − cumulative due = Σ of every card's advance in days): J1 is not a setting, it rises only when that spares longer advances later. Rejected: a tolerant rule (a plateau one day shorter than the one above), a cap on J1 (+1, +2), other criteria (largest share, fewest cards). The author also chose **the rule alone at the bottom** (no more emptying the last days to shorten the 1s, `even_out`'s preference of 2026-11-12): `Pistes d'album` 90 days `2×4, 1×86` instead of `2×31, 1×32, 0×27` (advance 34 vs 1627). Results 2026-11-16: `Bépo` 30/90/365 days → `19, 18, 17, 16×2 … 11×2, 10×3, 9×4, 8×6, 7×6, 6×6, 5×7, 4×7, 3×7, 2×12, 1×17…` (advance 149 instead of 592 at 90 days); `Géographie` unchanged at 30/90 days; cards moved at 365 days: `Géographie` 14 (was 653), `Pistes d'album` 25 (was 145). Algorithm: A* day by day over (day, level, days at it, days it must still last, largest next step, cumulative); pruned by dominance (same day, level, step, cumulative: shorter plateau, no longer wait, no more advance), by `keeps_up` (a curve never above its level must still reach the cards due), by the fastest descent (too many cards); bound = cost of the hull from the state **in whole cards** (floor of the least concave majorant, daily counts sorted, as `auto_curve`). The whole-card bound is what makes it fast: with the real-valued hull, `Bépo` over 365 days took 13–167 s (a sparse tail at 0.3 card/day costs nothing in real numbers, but whole non-increasing counts must stay at 1 then drop to 0); now 0.3 s. Worst seen: `Paroles` in auto mode, 2.4 s (365 days), 3.7 s (730). `RegularStaircaseTest` checks optimality against every non-increasing curve of small windows.
  - **Down to 0** (asked 2026-11-12): when the curve ends at 1 or 0 card/day, days at 0 are a level like the others and the 1s plateau gives them days while it is at least as long: they absorb the jumps no dip can. The author prefers more days at 3 and 2 and fewer at 1, **even if it leaves empty days at the end of the window** (`Paroles (entières)`: `3x20 2x28 1x40` → `3x26 2x26 1x26 0x10`).

  - **Rejected attempt** (don't retry): minimising the sum of squared differences of adjacent plateau lengths by local moves. It got stuck on Bépo's `9x2 8x4`, broke the 2026-11-11 example and lengthened peaks (`Paroles (entières)` `4x2` → `4x8`).
  - **Peak**: a block of several plateaus never gives days to the peak's plateau (repeated, `4x5 3x37 2x46 1x2` would become `4x22 3x23 2x23 1x22`). A single plateau still may, one day at a time.
  - Otherwise (curve ending at ≥ 2) the plateau at the last day's level is excluded, also as a neighbour: its length only reflects where the window ends (`Pays`'s `3x37 2x46` must stay).
  - **Rejected attempts** (don't retry): smoothing the daily rate with a moving average then re-hulling changes nothing (a moving average of a descending curve stays at or below it, and the hull snaps back to the due counts); rounding the smoothed cumulative *up* instead inflates the curve (`18, 18, 16, 16, 16` on Bépo, a 5 on Pays).
- `follow_curve(cards, horizon, curve, taken)` (custom curve, 2026-11-13): the window gets exactly `curve[day]` cards; if there are not enough cards (after `_outside_target` has taken what it can), the curve is cut from its end. Since 2026-11-14 the leftovers below no longer pile on horizon + 1: `_outside_target` sends them to the days after the window below the carried-on curve. `assign_both_ways`: like `assign_in_short_hops` but cards may also be **postponed** (each card may move ≤ s × interval either way, bisection on s; days filled from day 1, taking the allowed cards whose last allowed day comes first). Cards not needed: due after the window they stay; due in it they go to **horizon + 1** (a pile there, fixed by the next run — the author smooths daily). On the parent deck `Musique` (other subdecks than the sheet's) the sheet's curve postpones thousands of cards: the curve is meant for one deck.
- `fill_gaps(cards, horizon, taken)` (**removed 2026-11-16** at the author's request, with its method; kept here for its history and for `assign_in_short_hops`, born with it; asked 2026-11-13, method « Combler seulement les creux », was a checkbox for a day): the alternative to `smooth` that **keeps the current shape** and only fills gaps. Asked on `Musique::Pistes d'album` (90 days: `222 1×85 0 3`): `smooth` gave `2×31 1×33 0×26` (51 cards moved), the author wanted the 2s kept and only the empty day filled → now `2222 1×86`. Admittedly at odds with the regular staircase of `smooth`, hence an option. How: `levels(counts)` over the extended period = closest non-increasing curve (least squares, pool-adjacent-violators on averages), **rounded half up** per pooled block (`1, 1, 0, 3` → 1.25 → 1; `1, 2` → 1.5 → 2; a sparse tail `0, 0, 1` → 0.33 → 0, so no gap there). A gap = window day below its level; filled up to the level, **from the first gap to the last** (tried last-to-first first: on `Pistes d'album` over 365 days, late gaps used up the cards and left empty days in the middle; first-to-last leaves them at the end and halves the advances on `Géographie` 365 days: max 59 days / 14% of the interval instead of 296 / 69%). Each gap takes the nearest later card that can leave its day: days above their level (lowered at most to it, never into a new gap), or any card after the window (lookahead); longest interval first; not next to a sibling while another card can come, else the sibling rather than an empty day. **Then the window is made non-increasing** (asked the same day: after gap filling, J90 was still 2 above J89 = 1, and the author wanted J90 at 1 and J4 at 2, "généralisé"): while a day has more cards than the day before, one of its cards (longest interval, sibling rule) goes to the **first step down before it whose lower side is below that day's count** — a 2 after 1s lengthens the 2s plateau, a 1 after 0s fills the first empty day, `3 3 2 2 2 2 4` → `3 3 3 3 2…` (no new peak); J1 only if the curve is flat before the rise. First version took the first step down at any level: on `Pistes d'album` 365 days every isolated 1 of the sparse tail climbed to J4 (46 days at 2, advances up to 94% of the interval) — don't go back to it. Only advances; the postpone checkbox is disabled while it is on. **The steps above only set the curve; the cards that make it are then chosen by `assign_in_short_hops`** (asked the same day: rather than one card J90 → J4 at 30% of its interval, a chain of short hops J5 → J4, J6 → J5…, "pour que les pourcentages de déplacement soient plus faibles"). It minimises the **largest** advance relative to the interval (bisection on the share s; with s, each card may advance ≤ s × interval; days filled from day 1, each taking the allowed cards due soonest — its own first, so hops stay short), unlike `assign` which minimises the *sum* and therefore prefers the one long hop of the longest-interval card. **`smooth` uses it too since 2026-11-13** (author: "applique le même principe au lissage normal"); see `assign` below. Siblings: strictly avoided (a larger s is tried instead); ignored only if the curve is unreachable otherwise. Cards assigned after the window keep their due day. Trade-off accepted by asking: many more cards move. Results 2026-11-13 (curves as above): `Pistes d'album` 90 days 78 cards, avg 1.1 days, max 4 days / 1% (was 2 cards, max 30%); 365 days 284 cards, max 164 days / 30% (was 115, 44%); `Géographie` 365 days 599 cards, avg 10.3 days, max 28 days / 5% (was 152, 14%); 30/90 days nothing to move on either deck. Curves: `Géographie` 365 days → clean `5 4 3 2 1` staircase; `Pistes d'album` 90 days `2222 1×86`, 365 days `2222`, 1s up to about day 290, then 0s.
- **Compromise between short hops and few cards** (`fewer_moves`, all three methods, asked 2026-11-13): short hops alone moved hundreds of cards by 1 day for 2 missing cards (`Paroles de chansons`, 4719 cards for a 4721 curve over 365 days: 391 to 624 cards moved, max 0.2% of the interval). Now: the short-hops assignment gives the smallest possible largest share s*; then `_few_moves` allows each card to move up to `max(s*, ceiling)` of its interval (`MOVE_CEILING` = 5%, config `move_ceiling_percent`) and moves few cards — greedy from day 1: each day keeps its own cards (the least movable ones if too many); a short day takes first cards that must move anyway (left over from earlier days — postponing, custom curve only —, the nearest later day above its target, cards due after the window while the window needs some), else the **farthest** card it may take (a gap is carried in as few hops as the ceiling allows: Pistes d'album 90 days `(19,4) (34,19) … (90,79) (90,89)`, 7 cards at 5% instead of 86 at 0.3%). The greedy pass is not monotonic in the share (longer hops allowed can move more cards), so both s* and max(s*, ceiling) are tried and the fewest cards moved wins; if both fail or move more, the short hops stay. Tests: same curve, share ≤ max(s*, ceiling), never more cards than `ceiling=0`.
- `move_stats()`: count / mean / max days / max share of the interval, for advanced and for postponed cards. No wording here: sentences are built by `i18n.Translator.describe_moves()`.
- `assign(cards, target)`: fills days from the last one backwards, each day taking the available cards (due on or after it) with the **shortest interval** first. This minimises the total relative advance Σ (due − new_due) / interval — proven against brute force in the tests. Raises `InfeasibleCurve` if the target would need postponing. It may **chain moves** (e.g. a long-interval card J6 → J3 and a short-interval card J7 → J6 rather than J7 → J3 directly), so it touches more cards than strictly needed. Was kept on 2026-11-11 over a "fewest cards moved" variant (which moved each card further relative to its interval). **No longer used by `smooth` since 2026-11-13**: replaced by `assign_in_short_hops` (minimises the *largest* relative advance, in short hops — see `fill_gaps` above), at the author's request. More cards move, each much less. `assign` remains (tested, and the reference for the sum objective); don't switch back unless asked.

## Environment gotchas

- Python 3.13 was installed with winget on 2026-11-12 (Anki's bundled Python has no `python.exe`). A shell started before that install may still resolve `python` to the Microsoft Store stub: reload `PATH` from the registry or restart VS Code.
- The author applies smoothings and reviews between sessions: real-deck figures (including the examples in this file) go stale quickly. **Re-run `simulate.py` before quoting a deck's curve**; the dialog is the reference.
- AnkiConnect requests must be sent as UTF-8 bytes; non-ASCII deck names fail otherwise.
- AnkiConnect has no "today" action: `simulate.py` deduces Anki's day number from a card found with `prop:due=N`.
- Target Python: Anki 26.09 bundles Python 3.13, but keep the code compatible with older Anki versions' Python 3.9 (`from __future__ import annotations`, no `match`, no `X | Y` at runtime).
